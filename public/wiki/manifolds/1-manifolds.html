Atlases

Definition 5.1.1.1.

An atlas of a topological \(n\)-manifold \(M\) is a collection \(\{(U_\alpha, \phi_\alpha): \alpha \in I\}\) of charts such that \(U_\alpha\) are an open cover of \(M\). The maps \(\phi_\beta \circ \phi_\alpha^{-1}\) are the transition functions.

If the transition functions are smooth, we have a smooth atlas. Equivalent atlases are those such that their union is also a smooth atlas. A smooth structure on \(M\) is a vchoice of equivalence class1 of smooth atlases. This smooth structure makes \(M\) a smooth manifold.

Maps

A map \(f: M \to N\) is smooth if for any \(p \in M\), there is a coordinate chart \((U, \phi)\) around \(p\) in some atlas representing the smooth structure on \(M\) and a coordinate chart \((V, \psi)\) around \(f(p)\) in some atlas representing the smooth structure on \(N\) such that \(f(U) \subset V\) and \(g = \psi \circ f \circ \phi^{-1}: \phi(U) \to psi(V)\) is smooth. This does not depend on the choice of coordinate charts in a fixed atlas for \(M\) and \(N\).

\(f\) is a diffeomorphism if it is a smooth homeomorphism with smooth inverse. If the restriction of any \(f\) to \(U\) is a diffeomorphism from \(U \to f(U)\) then we say \(f\) is a local diffeomrphism.

We say \(f: U \to \mathbb R^n\) is a submersion at \(x \in U\) where \(U\) is an open subset of \(\mathbb R^m\) (and \(f\) is smooth) if it has surjective differential \(df_x\). Similarly immersions have injective differentials.

Now if \(f\) is a map between manifolds instead, we do this in terms of the coordinate charts: there must exist charts around \(p\) and \(f(p)\) in \(M\) and \(N\) respectively such that \(f(U) \subset V\) and \(g = \psi \circ f \circ phi^{-1}\) is a submersion at \(\phi(p)\), for example.

If \(f\) is a submersion and an immersion at \(p \in M\), then \(f\) is a local diffeomorphism at \(p\). We can also always pick a chart such that \(\phi(p) = 0\), and \(\psi(f(p)) = 0\) such that \(psi\circ f \circ phi^{-1}\) is the canonical submersion (or immersion) (these correspond to the usual coordinate projection and inclusions with extra zeroes).

If \(f\) is an immersion and \(f: M \to f(M)\) is a homeomorphism, then we say \(f\) is an embedding.

Let \(M\) be a subset of \(N\). Then \(M\) is a submanifold of \(N\) of dimension \(m leq n\) if for each \(p \in M\), there is a chart \((U, \phi)\) for \(N\) around \(P\) such that \(\phi(p) = 0\), and \(\phi(U \cap M) = \phi(U) \cap (\mathbb R^m \times \{0\})\).

A point \(p \in M\) is a critical point of \(f\) if \(f\) is not a submersion at \(p\). Then \(f(p)\) is a critical value: \(q \in N\) is a critical value if there is a \(p \in f(q)^{-1}\) such that \(p\) is a critical point. \(q \in N\) is otherwise a regular value if this is not true.

Orientation

\(M\) is orientable if there is a smooth atlas \(\Phi = \{(U_\alpha, \phi_\alpha), \alpha \in I\}\) representing the smooth structure on \(M\) such that for each \(\alpha, \beta \in I\) where \(U_\alpha \cap U_\beta \neq \emptyset\), the transition function \(\phi_\beta \circ \phi_\alpha^{-1}\) satisfies \[ \operatorname{det}(d(\phi_\beta \circ \phi_\alpha^{-1})_x) > 0\] for each \(x \in \phi_\alpha(U_\alpha \cap U_\beta)\).

An orientation is a choice of atlas satisfying the orientability condition on \(M\). If the union of two atlases satisfying the orientatbility criterion also satsifies it, they determine the same orientation on \(M\).
